Severity and case classification
SERIOUSNESS AND EXPECTEDNESS : 

 1) Seriousness (ICH E2A criteria)

A case is Serious if it meets any one of these:

Criterion	Notes

Death	- Outcome of the event

Life-threatening - At the time of the event, not "could theoretically become fatal"

Hospitalization or prolongation of existing stay -	Initial admission or extended stay caused by the event

Persistent/significant disability or incapacity	- Substantial disruption to normal life functions

Congenital anomaly/birth defect	- In offspring of exposed patient

Medically important event -	Doesn't fit above but required intervention to prevent one of them (e.g., ER treatment for bronchospasm, seizure, blood dyscrasia, drug dependence)

If none apply → Non-serious.

2) Expectedness

Compare against the Reference Safety Information (RSI) — the package insert/SmPC for marketed drugs, the Investigator's Brochure for investigational ones.

Expected: nature, severity, and specificity match what's already listed

Unexpected: Not listed at all, OR listed but this case is more severe/specific than described (e.g., label says "rash," case is "Stevens-Johnson syndrome" → unexpected)


CASE CLASSIFICATION : 

Case 1

Scenario: 68-year-old on warfarin develops major GI bleed requiring hospitalization and blood transfusion. Label lists "bleeding" as a known risk, but not specifically "GI bleed requiring transfusion" as an outcome category.

Seriousness criterion -	Hospitalization + medically important (transfusion) → Serious

Expectedness -	Bleeding is class-labeled but transfusion-requiring severity is arguably a different specificity → borderline; most SOPs would call this Expected since GI bleeding on anticoagulants is a well-characterized class effect

Classification - Serious/Expected

Clinical impact -	Routine periodic reporting; may trigger signal review if frequency increases


Case 2

Scenario: 34-year-old on a newly approved biologic develops anaphylaxis within 20 minutes of infusion, treated with epinephrine in the ER, discharged same day. Anaphylaxis not mentioned in current SmPC (only "infusion reactions, mild-moderate" listed).


Seriousness - criterion	Life-threatening + medically important event (ER treatment) → Serious

Expectedness -	Anaphylaxis is a different severity/specificity than "mild-moderate infusion reaction" → Unexpected

Causality -	Probable (temporal relationship, known mechanism plausibility)

Classification -	SUSAR

Clinical impact -	Expedited report 7-day timeline (life-threatening); notify IRB/ethics committee and regulatory authority; consider label update trigger
